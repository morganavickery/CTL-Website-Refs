Microsoft Teams offers a range of built-in and add-on tools for making live class sessions more interactive — from polls and Q&A to breakout rooms and collaborative whiteboards. This guide covers what's already available in every Teams meeting, plus third-party apps that can be added to extend those capabilities further.

Working draft for faculty recommendation.

Native Teams Meeting Features

Moderated question queue layered on meeting chat; students submit/upvote questions, presenter marks answered, exportable list.

Affordances / Benefits: Reduces chat clutter in large sessions; upvoting surfaces most-wanted questions; anonymous option; keeps a record for follow-up.

Caveats / Limitations: Only live/synchronous, no persistence as a discussion board; moderation queue can lag in very large classes; less robust than dedicated backchannel tools.

Concrete Use Cases:

  • Students type questions during a large lecture instead of interrupting; the instructor answers the top-voted ones at the end.
  • During a guest lecture, students submit questions throughout the talk so the guest can address common ones together.
  • At a PhD qualifying exam, the committee chair queues questions so only one person speaks at a time.

Live multiple-choice, word cloud, ranking, and quiz-style polls launched mid-meeting with instant results.

Affordances / Benefits: Deep customization of question types; built into the meeting UI with no extra login; live results build momentum for discussion.

Caveats / Limitations: Anonymity/reporting weaker than dedicated tools; hard to reuse across sections without recreation; limited longitudinal analytics.

Use Cases:

  • Mid-lecture, students vote on a sample problem so the instructor can see who understood it before moving on.
  • Before a debate, students vote anonymously on their initial position so the class can see how opinions are split.
  • A quick quiz poll at the start of a review session flags who is behind on the reading.

Presenter mode where attendees can view slides at their own pace with live captions and accessible, high-contrast viewing.

Affordances / Benefits: Meaningful accessibility gains (live/translated captions, screen-reader-friendly view); low friction since it is just "presenting" a normal deck.

Caveats / Limitations: Only helps if already lecturing from slides; adds no interactivity on its own; self-paced viewing can fragment shared attention.

Concrete Use Cases:

  • A deaf student reads live, auto-generated captions during lecture instead of needing a separate captioning service.
  • International students follow along using live translated captions in a language they're more comfortable with.
  • A student who got lost mid-lecture scrolls back through the exact slides at their own pace after class.

Surveys/quizzes built in Forms, can be "shared live" to the meeting screen for real-time response.

Affordances / Benefits: Auto-grading for quizzes; fallback when Polls' UI does not fit; exports cleanly to Excel for gradebook or analysis.

Caveats / Limitations: Less flashy real-time UI than Mentimeter/Kahoot; slower to set up mid-session than native Polls.

Concrete Use Cases:

  • Students submit a 2-question form before leaving class so the instructor knows what to revisit next time.
  • A short survey before a new unit shows what students already know, so class time skips what they've covered.
  • End-of-semester feedback is collected anonymously and exported to a spreadsheet to spot patterns.

Splits a meeting into smaller simultaneous rooms; instructor can pre-assign or randomize and drop into any room.

Affordances / Benefits: Core infrastructure for active-learning/peer-instruction pedagogy; instructor can visit rooms like a physical classroom.

Caveats / Limitations: No persistent state between sessions; limited instructor visibility across many rooms at once; setup/teardown eats meeting time.

Concrete Use Cases:

  • Groups of 4 discuss a patient case study for 10 minutes, then compare recommendations as a class.
  • Students pair up to practice a scripted conversation aloud before speaking in front of the whole class.
  • Each project team gets its own room for the full period while the instructor checks in on progress.

Video layouts compositing the presenter's camera feed with content.

Affordances / Benefits: Increases visual presence/engagement cues relative to a static screen-share; can help sustain attention in long sessions.

Caveats / Limitations: Bandwidth/hardware dependent; some faculty and students find it gimmicky; no direct evidence it improves learning outcomes.

Concrete Use Cases:

  • The instructor's video layers over the slides during a dramatic story, keeping focus on their expressions.
  • In a studio art demo, the instructor's hands and materials are shown next to the shared screen.
  • A guest speaker's video and slides appear side-by-side, making the session feel like a conversation.

Emotion check-ins (Class Teams "how are you feeling?" prompts), journaling prompts, aggregated wellbeing analytics for the instructor.

Affordances / Benefits: Low-friction way to gauge classroom climate; built natively into Class Teams; free.

Caveats / Limitations: Not appropriate for this audience: designed primarily around K-12 SEL frameworks; adoption and validated pedagogical evidence at the undergrad/grad level is thin; requires Class Team structure most higher-ed courses do not use.

Concrete Use Cases:

  • A weekly emoji check-in in a large intro course flags when many students are struggling before it shows up in grades.
  • After a stressful exam period, a private reflection prompt surfaces concerns students might not raise directly.
  • Weekly check-ins in a first-year seminar track whether students feel more confident as the term goes on.

Third-Party Teams Apps

Live polling, word clouds, Q&A; competitor to clickers. Free tier caps at roughly 25 responses.

Affordances / Benefits: Long track record in higher ed as a "clicker replacement"; strong peer-instruction research base; works via web link, no app install for students.

Caveats / Limitations: Free tier caps at roughly 25 responses per poll — workable for a small seminar, but a paid plan is needed for a real lecture-hall-sized class.

Concrete Use Cases:

  • Students vote on a conceptual question; if fewer than 70% answer correctly, the instructor re-explains before moving on.
  • Before a debate, students anonymously vote on their position so quieter students can weigh in honestly.
  • A live word cloud of one-word answers becomes the discussion starter for the day.
